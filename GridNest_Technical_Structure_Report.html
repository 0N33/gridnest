PILLAR I IOT HARDWARE, SENSORS & TELEMETRY INFRASTRUCTURE
HARDWARE-IN-THE-LOOP & EDGE INGESTION

1. Physical Hardware Sensor Suite & Benchtop Architecture

The physical sensing tier employs a dual-core 32-bit ESP32 microcontroller running real-time RMS signal processing routines across galvanic-isolated analog transducers. Current and potential signals are digitized at 1.0 kHz to sample complete 50 Hz AC sinusoidal waveforms, computing true RMS current (Irms), RMS voltage (Vrms), and apparent power (S).

NON-INVASIVE CURRENT SENSOR (SCT-013-000)
  • Rated Input: 0 to 100A AC primary line current.
  • Secondary Output: 0 to 50mA secondary current (split-core).
  • Burden Resistor: Precision 33 Ω metal-film resistor converting current output to 0–1.65V AC swing.
  • DC Bias Network: Dual 10kΩ voltage divider centering AC signal around 1.65V DC reference (ADC mid-rail).
ACTIVE VOLTAGE TRANSFORMER (ZMPT101B)
  • Rated Input: 0 to 250V AC single-phase mains supply.
  • Galvanic Isolation: 4000V dielectric breakdown barrier.
  • Signal Conditioning: On-board multi-turn cermet potentiometer adjusting LM358 operational amplifier gain.
  • Phase Accuracy: < 20 arc-minutes phase error at rated nominal voltage.

3. Physical Hardware Isolation vs. Virtual IoT Simulator Architecture

BENCHTOP HARDWARE DECOUPLING PRINCIPLE

To guarantee zero synthetic signal contamination, Building 4 (CONS_N_004) and Transformer 101 (TX_101) are decoupled from the synthetic load simulation loop whenever physical hardware telemetry is detected. Their virtual IoT simulator cards and controls are removed from the frontend and replaced by real-time hardware telemetry streams. The remaining 48 buildings and Transformer 102 remain driven by the multi-scenario diurnal virtual simulator (iot_simulator.py).

MASS-BALANCE AUDIT & FORENSIC DOSSIER

1. AC Electrical Physics Engine & Technical Loss Modeling

Real-world power systems naturally dissipate energy through resistive heating (I2R). An AI that flags every energy discrepancy without modeling physical losses causes rampant false positives. GridNest solves this by embedding Ohm's and Kirchhoff's laws directly into the digital twin (models/electrical.py).

SERVICE DROP VOLTAGE DROP EQUATION

Calculates voltage drop ΔV along the service line based on conductor resistance (R), reactance (X), active power (P), and reactive power (Q):

ΔV = (P · R + Q · X) / Vnom = I · (R cosφ + X sinφ) Vconsumer = Vtransformer - ΔV
TECHNICAL COPPER LOSS CALCULATION

Models line dissipation (Ploss) and transformer load and no-load (core) losses:

Pcopper = I2 · Rline = (Pload / (V · PF))2 · (r · d) PTX, loss = Pcore + Pcu · (Sload / Srated)2

2. Real-Time Mass-Balance Energy Accounting Engine (energy_accounting.py)

At every 15-minute billing window or tick cycle, the Energy Accounting Engine performs conservation-of-energy audits across each distribution transformer sector, isolating Non-Technical Loss (NTL):

NTLfeeder = [ EDT supplied - (∑ Emetered, i + Etechnical losses) ] / EDT supplied × 100%

1. Stage 1: Multi-Signal Behavioral Feature Extraction (26 Features)

Rather than feeding raw noisy meter timeseries into black-box neural nets, GridNest extracts 26 domain-engineered behavioral features that quantify human consumption consistency, cyclic autocorrelation, and statistical collapse:

ELECTRICAL LOAD FACTOR (LF)

Measures consumption consistency vs peak demand:

LF = μ / (maxt xt + 10-4)

Meter bypass for heavy ACs collapses average while leaving erratic peak spikes.

TEMPORAL AUTOCORRELATION (rk)

Quantifies daily (k=1) and weekly (k=7) human rhythm:

rk = ∑ (xt - μ)(xt-k - μ) / ∑ (xt - μ)2

Tampered meters disrupt human routines, yielding abnormal near-zero correlations.

VARIANCE COLLAPSE RATIO (Rσ)

Compares recent 30-day volatility to historical baseline:

Rσ = σ(Xrecent) / (σ(Xhistorical) + 10-4)

Sudden artificial freeze or meter slowdown causes Rσ → 0.

TRANSFORMER PEER RATIO (Rpeer)

Neighborhood parity check against shared transformer:

Rpeer = xi / (medianj ∈ TX(xj) + 10-4)

Identifies consumers consuming <15% of neighboring premises on the same phase.
